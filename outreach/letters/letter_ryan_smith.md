Dear Dr. Smith,

I have been following your work on fitting Active Inference to empirical data — especially the 2022 tutorial with Friston and Whyte, and the 2026 complexity-matched comparison with Chou, Hakimi, and Hsu. That comparison makes a point I have been trying to sharpen from the design side: overlapping predictive accuracy is not the same as a theory-specific information-seeking mechanism.

I developed a simulation-tested experimental design that addresses a structural identifiability problem in Active Inference. Standard symmetric information-seeking tasks allow a flexible curiosity model to become nearly observationally equivalent to an exact-information-gain controller. We then constructed matched asymmetric observation channels that hold prior probability, mean cue accuracy, and instrumental sampling value constant while varying exact mutual information. In the tested synthetic class this restores family discrimination against that curiosity family.

This is not a claim of a new design class. Nelson et al. (2010, Experiment 3, Condition 1) already tied probability gain while varying information gain and found only 12/22 participants preferred the higher-IG feature after experience-based learning. The live question is therefore whether an Active Inference decision framing adds anything empirically distinct beyond that older OED contrast — not whether matched information channels can be invented. A further equivalence analysis limits even a later positive result: it would support exact-MI-sensitive valuation relative to specified alternatives; it would not uniquely validate Active Inference or the Free Energy Principle.

The reduced controller is an EFE-derived epistemic-value component under stated assumptions, not a process-level implementation of full AIF. I am therefore not writing to claim a confirmation of the framework. I am writing because a more falsifiable information-seeking contrast seems aligned with the empirical programme your group has been arguing for.

I can share a 2-page brief, the v1.1 preprint, and a one-command reproduction of the shipped synthetic checks. I am looking for a collaborator with human behavioral-experiment infrastructure to co-develop a small feasibility pilot — learnability of asymmetric contingencies, heterogeneity, and heuristic substitution — and, if warranted, later freeze a confirmatory protocol. I would contribute the design, computational models, and analysis pipeline; your group would contribute empirical task expertise, recruitment / ethics infrastructure, and joint interpretation.
